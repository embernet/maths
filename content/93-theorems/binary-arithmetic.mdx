## What it says

In **binary** (base 2), each integer is represented using only the digits 0 and 1 and place values that are powers of 2:

$$1011_2 = 1 \cdot 2^3 + 0 \cdot 2^2 + 1 \cdot 2^1 + 1 \cdot 2^0 = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11$$

Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division all work in binary using the same algorithms as in decimal, with carries of 2 rather than 10. Boolean logic (AND, OR, NOT) corresponds directly to binary arithmetic.

## History

[Leibniz](/mathematicians/leibniz) developed the binary number system around 1679 and published it in 1703 in a paper connecting it to Chinese hexagrams, which he saw as an ancient binary system. He found it philosophically significant — creation from nothing (0) by addition of the One (1). Boole's logic and Shannon's information theory gave it practical importance. Every digital computer since the 1940s represents all data in binary.

## Why it matters

Binary arithmetic is the foundation of digital computing: all integers, floating-point numbers, characters, and instructions in a computer are binary strings. Boolean algebra — which operates on bits — is the algebra of digital logic gates. Error-correcting codes, cryptographic operations, and quantum computing all operate on binary or higher-order generalisations.

## Real-world applications

[Quantum computing](/applications/quantum-computing-algorithms) uses qubits (quantum binary digits). [Error-correcting codes](/applications/error-correcting-codes) operate on binary vectors. [RSA cryptography](/applications/rsa-public-key-cryptography) performs modular arithmetic on binary representations of integers.
