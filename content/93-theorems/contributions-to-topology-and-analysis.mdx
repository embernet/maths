## What it says

[Kolmogorov](/mathematicians/kolmogorov)'s topological and analytic contributions include:

- **Kolmogorov superposition theorem (1957):** Every continuous function of $n$ variables can be written as a superposition of continuous functions of a single variable. This answered Hilbert's 13th problem: is it possible to express a function of three variables as a composition of functions of two variables? Kolmogorov showed that even continuous functions of two variables suffice.

- **Contributions to cohomology (1936):** Kolmogorov independently discovered the cohomology ring of a topological space.

- **Approximation theory:** Work on how well functions can be approximated by polynomials, connecting to information-theoretic entropy of function classes.

## History

Kolmogorov had extraordinary breadth. His superposition theorem surprised mathematicians and, later, attracted attention from neural network researchers — it can be interpreted as saying that a certain two-layer network can represent any continuous function, though the connection is more subtle than it first appears.

## Why it matters

The superposition theorem has implications for dimensionality reduction and function approximation. The cohomology work was a rare case of a major topological discovery being made independently by a probabilist, showing the deep connections between analysis and topology.

## Real-world applications

Kolmogorov's approximation theory informs the theoretical study of [machine learning](/applications/machine-learning-deep-neural-networks) model expressiveness.
