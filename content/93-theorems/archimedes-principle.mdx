        The buoyant force on an immersed body equals the weight of the fluid it displaces. If the displaced weight exceeds the body's own weight, the body rises; otherwise it sinks.

## History

[Archimedes](/mathematicians/archimedes) published the principle in *On Floating Bodies* (c.250 BCE). Legend has him leaping from his bath and running naked through the streets of Syracuse shouting "Eureka!" — having just realised he could detect adulteration of the king's gold crown by submerging it and measuring the displaced water. The story is almost certainly embellished, but Archimedes's actual treatise was rigorous and quantitative.

## Why it matters

It is the first quantitative law of fluid statics and the foundation of naval architecture. The reasoning by which Archimedes derived it — comparing actual and imagined fluid configurations to extract a static-equilibrium argument — anticipates the variational principles of analytical mechanics by two thousand years.

## Where you'll meet it

A historical anchor for the fluid-statics module of any fluid-mechanics course.
