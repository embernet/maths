## What it says

Bolzano's 1817 paper *Rein analytischer Beweis* ("Pure analytical proof") contains:
- The first rigorous proof of the Intermediate Value Theorem
- An early formulation of what we now call the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of continuity
- The observation that a function should be proved continuous by logical deduction from its definition, not by appeal to geometric intuition

The paper predates Cauchy's *Cours d'analyse* (1821) and independently arrives at many of the same rigorous methods.

## History

[Bolzano](/mathematicians/bolzano) was a Bohemian mathematician and philosopher working largely in isolation from the main centres of mathematical activity (Paris, Berlin). His work was published but not widely circulated; Cauchy's parallel development, with the prestige of the École Polytechnique behind it, had far greater influence. Weierstrass later acknowledged Bolzano's priority. Bolzano is now recognised as one of the pioneers of rigorous analysis, decades before the [rigour revolution](/history/06-the-rigour-revolution) was in full swing.

## Why it matters

Bolzano showed that the foundations of analysis could be built on logical definitions and proofs rather than geometric pictures and physical intuition. His work is an early example of the mathematical virtues that would define the nineteenth-century programme: precision, rigour, and proof.

## Real-world applications

No direct applications, but the analytical foundations he helped establish underpin all of quantitative science.
