## What it says

Fisher's 1918 paper "The correlation between relatives on the supposition of Mendelian inheritance" resolved a major puzzle: Mendelian genetics (discrete traits, 3:1 ratios) seemed incompatible with the continuous variation (height, weight, intelligence) that Darwin's natural selection required. Fisher showed that if many independent genetic loci each contribute a small additive effect, the result is a continuous Gaussian distribution — exactly what is observed.

This explained: the correlation between relatives (parents and children share half their genetic loci on average), the heritability of traits, and the response to selection. It founded quantitative genetics as a discipline.

## History

[Fisher](/mathematicians/fisher) published this in 1918, aged 28, while a schoolteacher. It was the founding paper of quantitative genetics and a key component of the "modern evolutionary synthesis" that unified genetics and evolution in the 1930s–40s.

## Why it matters

Fisher's quantitative genetics framework is still the basis of animal and plant breeding, and of genome-wide association studies (GWAS) that identify genetic variants associated with complex human traits.
