## What it says

For a right-angled triangle with legs $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$:

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

This is a statement about lengths, but it is equally a statement about areas — the square built on $c$ has exactly the same area as the two squares built on $a$ and $b$ combined. That geometric reading is how the ancient world understood it, and it remains the most vivid way to see why it must be true.

## History

The result was known to Babylonian scribes well before [Pythagoras](/mathematicians/pythagoras) — the clay tablet Plimpton 322 (c. 1800 BCE) lists Pythagorean triples systematically. The Pythagorean school (c. 570–495 BCE) gave it the proof-based treatment that became canonical in Western mathematics. [Euclid](/mathematicians/euclid) recorded six distinct proofs in the *Elements* (Book I, Proposition 47). There are now over 370 known proofs, including one published in 1876 by a US Congressman named James Garfield — later the twentieth US President.

## Why it matters

Before Pythagoras' theorem, measuring distances required physical measurement. With it, you can compute any distance from coordinates alone. That shift — from direct measurement to algebraic computation — underlies the whole of analytic geometry and, through that, virtually every branch of quantitative science. The theorem is also the seed of Euclidean distance, inner products, and the structure of Hilbert spaces, which you will meet in specialist areas functional analysis.

## Real-world applications

Distance computation is everywhere. [GPS positioning](/applications/gps-positioning) triangulates your location by solving sets of Pythagorean-style distance equations against satellite positions. [Computer graphics](/applications/computer-graphics-3d) uses it at every frame to compute depths and normals. [Structural engineering](/applications/bridge-and-structural-engineering) needs it to resolve forces in trusses and beams. [Robotics](/applications/robotics-and-control) relies on it constantly for path planning and workspace geometry.

## Where you'll meet it

You will see Pythagoras' theorem in the [Classical Greece era](/90-history/02-classical-greece) and throughout [Euclidean geometry](/theorems/euclidean-geometry). It reappears whenever you encounter an inner-product space — the distance formula $\|v\| = \sqrt{v_1^2 + \cdots + v_n^2}$ is Pythagoras in $n$ dimensions.
