## What it says

The **Ramanujan tau function** $\tau(n)$ is defined by the generating function:

$$\Delta(q) = q\prod_{n=1}^\infty(1-q^n)^{24} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \tau(n) q^n$$

where $q = e^{2\pi i z}$ and $z$ is in the upper half-plane. The function $\Delta(z)$ is a **cusp form of weight 12** for the modular group $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Ramanujan's conjectures (1916):**
1. $\tau$ is multiplicative: $\tau(mn) = \tau(m)\tau(n)$ for $\gcd(m,n)=1$
2. For prime $p$: $|\tau(p)| \leq 2p^{11/2}$ (the Ramanujan conjecture)

The first was proved by Mordell (1917). The second — equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis for the Ramanujan zeta function — was proved by Deligne (1974) as a corollary of his proof of the Weil conjectures (a Fields Medal).

## History

[Ramanujan](/mathematicians/ramanujan) found these conjectures by direct computation. The proof required Grothendieck's algebraic geometry and l-adic cohomology — mathematical machinery that did not exist when Ramanujan wrote them.

## Why it matters

Ramanujan's delta function is the prototype for modular forms: automorphic objects that encode deep arithmetic. The connection between modular forms and elliptic curves — the Taniyama–Shimura theorem — is the key to Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

## Real-world applications

No direct applications, but modular forms are central to modern number theory and indirectly affect [RSA-type cryptography](/applications/rsa-public-key-cryptography) through elliptic curve theory.
