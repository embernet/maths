## What it says

A **ring** $R$ is a set with two operations (addition and multiplication) satisfying:
- $(R, +)$ is an abelian group
- Multiplication is associative and distributes over addition
- (For a unital ring: there exists a multiplicative identity $1$)

Examples: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ (matrices), $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

A **module** over a ring $R$ is an abelian group with a compatible $R$-action — the generalisation of a vector space from fields to rings.

[Noether](/mathematicians/noether)'s programme: study rings through their ideals (kernels of homomorphisms), study modules through their submodule structure, and classify everything by abstract properties.

## History

Noether's 1921 paper "Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen" ("Ideal theory in ring domains") reorganised algebraic number theory and invariant theory into a clean abstract framework. Her approach — identifying the axiomatic conditions that made theorems work, then proving the theorems from those conditions alone — defined what abstract algebra means.

## Why it matters

Noether's structural approach is now the standard mode of algebra: you define a type of algebraic structure by axioms, prove general theorems, then identify which structures satisfy the axioms. This is why all abstract algebra courses have the same shape: groups, rings, fields, modules. The approach applies across geometry, number theory, and topology.

## Real-world applications

[Error-correcting codes](/applications/error-correcting-codes) are modules over polynomial rings. [RSA cryptography](/applications/rsa-public-key-cryptography) uses ring arithmetic.
