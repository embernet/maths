## What it says

**Newton's three laws of motion** (*Principia Mathematica*, 1687):
1. A body at rest or uniform motion stays so unless acted upon by a force.
2. Force equals mass times acceleration: $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$.
3. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

**Law of universal gravitation:**
$$F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

Gravitational force between two masses $m_1, m_2$ separated by distance $r$ is proportional to both masses and inversely proportional to $r^2$. This was the first *universal* physical law — the same formula governs the Moon's orbit and a falling apple.

## History

[Newton](/mathematicians/newton) published *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* in 1687, deriving from these laws Kepler's three laws of planetary motion, the shape of the Earth, tidal patterns, and the precession of the equinoxes. He developed the calculus specifically to express and solve the differential equations involved.

## Why it matters

Newton's laws turned mechanics into a predictive mathematical science. For two centuries they were the complete description of physical reality — only at very high speeds (special relativity) and at atomic scales (quantum mechanics) do they fail. For engineering, astronomy, and most of physics, they remain exact enough.

## Real-world applications

[GPS positioning](/applications/gps-positioning) uses Newtonian orbital mechanics for satellite position prediction. [Robotics](/applications/robotics-and-control) uses Newton's laws for dynamics. [Gravitational-wave detection](/applications/gravitational-wave-detection) requires general relativity for the waves themselves but Newtonian mechanics for the detector response.
