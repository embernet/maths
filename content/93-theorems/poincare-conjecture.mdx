## What it says

A space is **simply connected** if every loop in it can be continuously shrunk to a point (the fundamental group is trivial). A **closed 3-manifold** is a compact 3-dimensional space without boundary.

**The Poincaré conjecture:** If a closed 3-manifold is simply connected, it is homeomorphic (topologically equivalent) to the 3-sphere $S^3$.

Analogues in dimension 2 were easy (there is only the 2-sphere). The conjecture in dimension 4 was proved by Freedman (1982, Fields Medal). In dimension 5 and above it was proved by Smale (1961, Fields Medal). Dimension 3 was the hardest and the last.

## History

[Poincaré](/mathematicians/poincare) posed the conjecture in 1904 — actually initially in the wrong direction and then corrected it. It was one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems (\$1 million each). Grigori Perelman proved it in 2002–03 using Ricci flow with surgery (a technique developed by Richard Hamilton). Perelman declined both the Fields Medal and the Millennium Prize.

## Why it matters

The Poincaré conjecture is now a theorem — and its proof introduced Ricci flow as a powerful tool in geometric analysis. The proof gives a complete topological classification of simply connected 3-manifolds and connects topology to geometry in a deep way.

## Real-world applications

No direct engineering applications, but the geometric analysis methods Perelman used are influencing differential geometry and mathematical physics.
