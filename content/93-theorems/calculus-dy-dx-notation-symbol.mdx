## What it says

[Leibniz](/mathematicians/leibniz) invented calculus independently of Newton in the 1670s, and his notation is what survived. The derivative of $y$ with respect to $x$ is written $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, treating $dy$ and $dx$ as infinitesimal increments. The integral is written $\int f(x)\,dx$, where the elongated $\int$ (a stylised $S$ for *summa*) denotes a continuous sum of infinitesimal area strips.

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus in Leibniz's notation:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a) \quad \text{where } F'(x) = f(x)$$

The notation makes the chain rule transparent: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du} \cdot \dfrac{du}{dx}$, with the $du$'s cancelling like fractions (they don't literally cancel, but the notation guides intuition correctly).

## History

Leibniz published his differential calculus in 1684 and integral calculus in 1686, several years after developing the ideas privately. [Newton](/mathematicians/newton) had similar results from the 1660s but published much later. The priority dispute that followed was bitter, nationalistic, and ultimately damaging to both parties' legacies. The question of independent invention is now settled: both developed calculus genuinely independently. History gave Leibniz's notation the victory because it is simply better for computation — you can manipulate $dy/dx$ algebraically in ways that Newton's $\dot{x}$ notation does not easily support.

## Why it matters

Good notation is not cosmetic. Leibniz's $dy/dx$ lets you treat derivatives as fractions in contexts (like the chain rule and separation of variables) where that intuition is sound. The $\int$ symbol became the universal shorthand for integration across all of science and engineering. Every formula in thermodynamics, electromagnetism, and quantum mechanics is written in Leibniz's language.

## Real-world applications

[Option pricing](/applications/option-pricing-finance) uses stochastic differential equations written entirely in Leibniz notation. [Signal processing](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3) integrates over time and frequency.
