## What it says

**Law of the lever:** Equal weights on a lever balance when their distances from the fulcrum are equal; unequal weights balance when their distances are inversely proportional to their weights. In modern terms, torques balance: $F_1 d_1 = F_2 d_2$.

**Archimedes' principle (buoyancy):** A body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid is buoyed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

Combining: a floating body displaces its own weight of fluid.

## History

[Archimedes](/mathematicians/archimedes) stated the law of the lever in *On the Equilibrium of Planes* and buoyancy in *On Floating Bodies*. The story of the crown and the bath (Archimedes leaping out shouting "Eureka!") is probably apocryphal but captures the spirit: buoyancy lets you measure the density of an irregular object without knowing its volume directly. These were the first mathematical mechanics — physical laws stated with proof and applied quantitatively.

## Why it matters

These are the foundations of statics and hydrostatics: the study of forces in balance. The lever principle is the basis of all mechanical advantage calculations. Buoyancy is the operating principle of ships, submarines, hot-air balloons, and any floating or submerged object. More abstractly, Archimedes' programme of mathematical mechanics — deriving physical results from mathematical principles with proof — is the model for theoretical physics.

## Real-world applications

[Structural engineering](/applications/bridge-and-structural-engineering) uses the lever principle in force and moment calculations. [Aerodynamics](/applications/airfoil-and-aerodynamics) uses Archimedes' principle in the analysis of lift in fluids.
