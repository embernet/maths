        The result connects two definitions of compactness: the open-cover version (every open cover admits a finite subcover) and the elementary version (closed and bounded). In $\mathbb{R}^n$ these are equivalent.

## History

The theorem owes its name to Eduard Heine, who used the property implicitly in 1872 while studying uniform continuity, and to Émile Borel, who isolated and proved it in his thesis of 1895. The general topological notion of compactness — born of this theorem — was developed by Fréchet and Hausdorff in the 1900s.

## Why it matters

Compactness is one of the most powerful conditions in analysis: continuous functions on compact sets are bounded, attain their extrema, are uniformly continuous, and so on. Heine-Borel is the concrete tool you use in $\mathbb{R}^n$ before you have the general topology to lean on.

## Where you'll meet it

In the second-year real-analysis course, as the bridge from elementary sequences to topological reasoning.
