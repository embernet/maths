## What it says

[Erdős](/mathematicians/erdos) was not primarily a theorem or formula but a mathematician whose style of work was itself significant. He had no fixed home, travelling constantly between universities and mathematicians, always carrying a suitcase, asking "Is your brain open?" before launching into a problem.

His 509 direct collaborators generated the **Erdős number**: a measure of collaborative distance from Erdős. Erdős himself has Erdős number 0; direct collaborators have number 1; collaborators of collaborators have number 2; and so on. The average mathematician has an Erdős number under 8.

## History

Erdős published his first paper in 1932 at age 18 and continued publishing until his death in 1996. He published papers in more fields than almost any other mathematician — combinatorics, number theory, set theory, graph theory, probability, geometry. He often proposed problems with cash prizes attached. Many remain open.

## Why it matters

Erdős represents a model of mathematics as a social enterprise: knowledge grows through collaboration, communication, and the free sharing of problems. His influence on the culture of mathematics — especially combinatorics and discrete mathematics — is enormous. The Erdős number network is one of the few concrete ways to visualise the collaborative structure of mathematical research.

## Real-world applications

No direct applications, but the graph-theoretic and combinatorial methods Erdős championed are fundamental to [network analysis](/applications/pagerank-google), [recommender systems](/applications/recommender-systems), and [cybersecurity](/applications/cybersecurity-anomaly-detection).
