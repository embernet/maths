        Let $H$ be a Hilbert space, $a: H \times H \to \mathbb{R}$ a bounded coercive bilinear form, and $L \in H^*$ a bounded linear functional. Then there is a unique $u \in H$ with $a(u, v) = L(v)$ for every $v \in H$.

## History

Peter Lax and Arthur Milgram published the result in 1954. It is the abstract framework that justifies the modern weak formulation of elliptic PDEs — and therefore the finite-element method.

## Why it matters

Weak formulations replace classical PDE problems with variational ones on Sobolev spaces. Lax-Milgram tells you when such a weak problem has a unique solution. It is the workhorse behind every finite-element analysis used in engineering.

## Real-world applications

Every [stress analysis of a bridge or aircraft wing](/applications/bridge-and-structural-engineering) and every adaptive mesh in [climate modelling](/applications/climate-modelling) rests on Lax-Milgram or one of its generalisations.

## Where you'll meet it

In the third-year functional-analysis course and in any first course on the finite-element method.
