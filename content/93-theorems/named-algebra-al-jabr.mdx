## What it says

Al-Khwarizmi's treatise *al-Kitāb al-mukhtaṣar fī ḥisāb al-jabr wa-l-muqābala* ("The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing," c. 820 CE) gave systematic methods for solving six types of linear and quadratic equations. The operation *al-jabr* ("completion") refers to adding a term to both sides of an equation to eliminate negative quantities; *al-muqābala* ("balancing") refers to collecting like terms.

The approach is rhetorical — written in words, not symbols — but fully general: the solutions are given as procedures that work for all values of the coefficients.

## History

[Al-Khwarizmi](/mathematicians/al-khwarizmi) worked at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad in the early ninth century. His algebra treatise was translated into Latin in the twelfth century and became a standard textbook in European universities for centuries. The word "algebra" entered European languages directly from *al-jabr*. The word "algorithm" entered via the Latinised form of his name (*Algoritmi*). Both words are now universal in mathematics and computing.

## Why it matters

Al-Khwarizmi's treatise represented the first fully systematic treatment of equation-solving: not particular numerical examples but general procedures applicable to any instance of a type of equation. This shift to systematic method is the essence of algebra. The Greek tradition (Diophantus) had brilliant particular solutions; al-Khwarizmi provided general algorithms.

## Real-world applications

Every systematic solution procedure in mathematics, computing, and [cryptography](/applications/rsa-public-key-cryptography) descends conceptually from the algorithmic tradition al-Khwarizmi founded.
