## What it says

[Pythagoras](/mathematicians/pythagoras) (or the Pythagorean school) observed that pleasing musical intervals correspond to simple ratios of string lengths:
- **Octave:** 2:1 (double the length, halve the frequency → octave lower)
- **Perfect fifth:** 3:2
- **Perfect fourth:** 4:3
- **Major third:** 5:4

The **harmonic series** — the sequence $f, 2f, 3f, 4f, \ldots$ of integer multiples of a fundamental frequency — is the set of pure tones a vibrating string produces simultaneously. The consonant intervals are those whose frequency ratios are ratios of small integers.

## History

This is one of the oldest known examples of a mathematical law governing a natural phenomenon. The Pythagoreans took it as evidence that "all is number" — that the universe has a mathematical structure. The equal temperament system (Bach's era) slightly violates pure Pythagorean ratios to allow transposition between keys.

## Why it matters

Pythagorean ratios connect mathematics to the physical world and to human perception in one of the most elegant ways. The fact that the harmonic series — $f, 2f, 3f, \ldots$ — is precisely what a Fourier analysis of a plucked string produces connects this ancient observation to the modern theory of [Fourier series](/theorems/fourier-series). Consonant intervals sound pleasant because they produce simple interference patterns.

## Real-world applications

[Signal processing and audio compression](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3) (MP3) exploits the Fourier structure of musical sounds that Pythagoras first observed.
