## What it says

Erdős made transformative contributions to both fields:

**Graph theory:** The Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem (1961) answers: how many $k$-element subsets of an $n$-element set can be chosen so that every two subsets share at least one element? Answer: at most $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$. Turán-type problems (maximum edges with no $k$-clique) and extremal graph theory more broadly.

**Number theory:** Erdős gave an elementary proof of the Prime Number Theorem (with Selberg, 1949) — the first proof avoiding complex analysis. Work on prime gaps, abundant numbers, and the Erdős–Strauss conjecture ($\frac{4}{n} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}$ for positive integers) remains open.

## History

[Erdős](/mathematicians/erdos) began his career in combinatorial number theory and graph theory and never left. His papers typically use elementary methods — no heavy machinery — but with extraordinary ingenuity. Many open problems he posed (with cash prizes ranging from \$25 to \$10,000) have driven research for decades.

## Why it matters

Erdős kept combinatorics and graph theory connected to elementary methods and intuition during a period when much of mathematics moved towards heavy abstraction. His results are often tight — best possible — and beautiful.

## Real-world applications

Graph theory underpins [PageRank](/applications/pagerank-google) and [recommender systems](/applications/recommender-systems). Extremal graph theory appears in [error-correcting code](/applications/error-correcting-codes) design.
