## What it says

An **elliptic integral** is one of the form $\int R(x, \sqrt{P(x)})\,dx$ where $P$ is a polynomial of degree 3 or 4 with no repeated roots. These cannot generally be expressed in terms of elementary functions — a discovery that motivated an entirely new theory.

[Abel](/mathematicians/abel) studied the *inverses* of elliptic integrals, defining **elliptic functions** as the meromorphic functions that invert these integrals. Crucially, elliptic functions are **doubly periodic**: there exist complex periods $\omega_1, \omega_2$ (linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$) such that $f(z + \omega_1) = f(z) = f(z + \omega_2)$. This is impossible for ordinary trigonometric functions, which have only one period.

The theory of elliptic curves — algebraic curves defined by $y^2 = P(x)$ for cubic $P$ — is the modern heir of this subject.

## History

Euler and Legendre had studied elliptic integrals, but Abel and Jacobi (independently, in 1827–29) made the decisive move of inverting them. Abel sent a 500-page memoir to the Paris Academy in 1826; it was mislaid and found only after his death. The theory he and Jacobi developed became one of the richest areas of nineteenth-century mathematics. It connects directly to [Fermat's Last Theorem](/theorems/fermat-s-last-theorem) — Wiles's proof used the theory of elliptic curves.

## Why it matters

Elliptic functions are the building blocks of a vast theory connecting complex analysis, algebraic geometry, and number theory. Elliptic curves over finite fields are now central to modern cryptography (ECC, used in most current TLS implementations). The theory of modular forms — a generalisation of elliptic functions — was the key to Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

## Real-world applications

The geodesic computations in [GPS positioning](/applications/gps-positioning) on an ellipsoid involve elliptic integrals. [Gravitational-wave detection](/applications/gravitational-wave-detection) signal templates use elliptic functions in the modelling of compact binary orbits.
