## What it says

Hilbert's *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (Foundations of Geometry, 1899) replaced Euclid's informal axioms with a rigorous modern axiom system. His axioms are grouped into five families:
1. **Incidence axioms** (e.g., two distinct points determine a unique line)
2. **Order axioms** (betweenness — what it means for a point to be between two others)
3. **Congruence axioms** (equality of lengths and angles)
4. **Parallel axiom** (the Playfair form of the parallel postulate)
5. **Continuity axioms** (Archimedean and completeness axioms)

Hilbert proved the system is consistent (by constructing a model using real numbers), complete for Euclidean geometry, and that the axioms are independent (no axiom follows from the others).

## History

Euclid's *Elements* had gaps that nobody had noticed for 2000 years: Euclid used betweenness without axiomatising it, and made "obvious" geometric inferences that required proof. [Hilbert](/mathematicians/hilbert)'s *Grundlagen* closed these gaps systematically. The book went through 14 editions.

## Why it matters

Hilbert showed that geometry, like arithmetic, can be made fully rigorous. The model-theoretic approach — prove consistency by finding a model — became standard. The book also demonstrated that Euclid's parallel postulate is genuinely independent of the other axioms, by constructing models (both Euclidean and non-Euclidean).

## Real-world applications

Modern [computer graphics](/applications/computer-graphics-3d) and [structural engineering](/applications/bridge-and-structural-engineering) use axiom-based computational geometry.
