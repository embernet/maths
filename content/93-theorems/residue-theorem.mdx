        For a meromorphic function $f$ with finitely many poles inside a closed positively-oriented contour $\gamma$:

$$
\oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_k \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k),
$$

where the residue $\operatorname{Res}(f, z_k)$ is the $-1$ coefficient of the Laurent expansion of $f$ at $z_k$.

## History

[Cauchy](/mathematicians/cauchy) developed the calculus of residues in the 1820s as he was building the rigorous theory of complex integration. The residue theorem packaged everything: Cauchy's integral theorem, Cauchy's integral formula, and the techniques for computing improper real integrals.

## Why it matters

It transforms hard real integrals into algebraic computations of residues. Definite integrals like $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{1}{1+x^4}\, dx$ that resist all elementary techniques fall in seconds. It is also the engine inside the inverse Laplace and Fourier transforms.

## Real-world applications

Inverse transforms in [signal processing](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3) and the recovery of images from MRI data both reduce to residue calculations.
