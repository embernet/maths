## What it says

The **heat equation** in one spatial dimension is:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$$

where $u(x, t)$ is the temperature at position $x$ and time $t$, and $\alpha > 0$ is the thermal diffusivity.

[Fourier](/mathematicians/fourier)'s insight: solutions can be written as superpositions of harmonics $e^{-\alpha n^2 t} \sin(nx)$. Each mode decays exponentially; high-frequency modes decay fastest, smoothing the temperature distribution over time. The general solution with initial condition $u(x, 0) = f(x)$ is:

$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n e^{-\alpha n^2 t} \sin(nx)$$

where $b_n$ are the Fourier sine coefficients of $f$.

## History

Fourier presented the heat equation and its Fourier series solution to the Paris Academy in 1807. The paper was rejected at first — Lagrange objected that Fourier series could not represent arbitrary functions. Fourier published the full theory in *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* (1822). The controversy about when and how Fourier series converge drove decades of research in analysis.

## Why it matters

The heat equation is the simplest and most important parabolic PDE. Its solution method — separating variables and expanding in Fourier modes — is the template for solving many PDEs. The same equation (with different physical interpretations of $u$) governs diffusion of chemicals, spread of heat, and the evolution of probability distributions in stochastic processes.

## Real-world applications

[Climate modelling](/applications/climate-modelling) and [weather forecasting](/applications/weather-forecasting) solve diffusion-type equations for heat and moisture in the atmosphere. [MRI](/applications/medical-imaging-mri-ct) involves diffusion-weighted imaging based on the heat equation.
