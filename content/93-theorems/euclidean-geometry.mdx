## What it says

Euclidean geometry describes points, lines, circles, and planes in a space where the parallel postulate holds: through any point not on a given line, there is exactly one line parallel to it. From this and four other postulates, all the familiar results of school geometry follow — the angle sum in a triangle is $180°$, similar triangles have proportional sides, and the Pythagorean relation $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ holds for right-angled triangles.

The distance between two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ in the Euclidean plane is:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

This formula is [Pythagoras' theorem](/theorems/pythagoras-theorem) in coordinate form, and it generalises to any number of dimensions.

## History

[Euclid](/mathematicians/euclid) codified this geometry around 300 BCE in the *Elements*. For two millennia it was geometry — the only geometry anyone knew or imagined. The cracks appeared in the nineteenth century when mathematicians realised that the parallel postulate could be negated without contradiction. [Gauss](/mathematicians/gauss) suspected this privately; Bolyai and Lobachevsky published hyperbolic geometry openly in the 1830s; [Riemann](/mathematicians/riemann)'s 1854 habilitation lecture unified the picture, showing Euclidean geometry as just one member of a family of geometries parameterised by curvature.

## Why it matters

Euclidean geometry remained the model of rigorous deductive reasoning for two thousand years, shaping philosophy, science, and architecture. Its overthrow in the nineteenth century was one of the great intellectual liberations in mathematics: geometry need not describe physical space; it is a formal structure, and multiple consistent geometries can coexist. That shift opened the door to the curved spacetime geometry of Einstein's general relativity.

## Real-world applications

Wherever space is locally flat — engineering, [structural design](/applications/bridge-and-structural-engineering), [3D computer graphics](/applications/computer-graphics-3d), [robotics](/applications/robotics-and-control) — Euclidean geometry is the working model. [GPS](/applications/gps-positioning) and surveying use it for most practical calculations, switching to spherical geometry only over large distances.
