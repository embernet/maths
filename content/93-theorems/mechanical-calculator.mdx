## What it says

The **Pascaline** (1642) is a mechanical adding machine: a series of interlocking gears where carrying is propagated automatically. When a gear completes a full revolution (representing 9 → 10), it advances the next gear by one tooth. This mimics the carry mechanism of manual addition.

Pascal built about 20 of the devices, of which about 9 survive. They could add and subtract; multiplication and division required repeated addition. The design was refined by Leibniz (the Stepped Reckoner, 1673), and mechanical calculating machines remained the dominant computing devices until electronic computers in the 1940s.

## History

[Pascal](/mathematicians/pascal) built the Pascaline at age 19 to help his father (a tax collector) perform repetitive arithmetic. Despite the elegance of the mechanism, the devices were too expensive and unreliable for widespread commercial use.

## Why it matters

The Pascaline represents the beginning of the idea that arithmetic can be mechanised — that a machine can perform calculations that previously required human thought. The Pascaline → Babbage's Difference Engine → ENIAC → modern computer is a direct conceptual lineage. Pascal's device is also the first commercially produced mechanical calculator.

## Real-world applications

Every digital computer is the descendant of Pascal's insight that arithmetic can be mechanised. Direct applications: all.
