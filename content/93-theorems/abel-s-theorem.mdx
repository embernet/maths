## What it says

**Abel's theorem on power series:** If $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n$ converges to $S$, then:

$$\lim_{x \to 1^-} \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n = S$$

More generally, if $\sum a_n r^n$ converges at $x = r$ (the endpoint of the radius of convergence), then the power series $f(x) = \sum a_n x^n$ has a limit equal to the series sum as $x \to r$ from inside the radius.

This fills in a gap in the theory of power series: convergence of a series and convergence of a function are not the same thing, and Abel's theorem clarifies the relationship at the boundary.

## History

[Abel](/mathematicians/abel) published this theorem in 1826 in *Crelle's Journal* as part of his campaign to put analysis on rigorous foundations. He was dismayed by Cauchy's 1821 "theorem" that the sum of a convergent series of continuous functions is continuous — Abel found counterexamples and showed the correct statement required uniform convergence (though he did not use that term). Abel died in 1829 at age 26; [Galois](/mathematicians/galois) died three years later at 20. Both left mathematics transformed.

## Why it matters

Abel's theorem is a prototype for results about interchange of limits — can you swap a limit and a sum? Usually not without care. The theorem establishes conditions under which the interchange is valid at the boundary, and the idea generalises to Abel summation, a technique for assigning sums to divergent series in a controlled way. Abel summation appears in analytic number theory and the study of the [Riemann zeta function](/theorems/riemann-zeta-function).

## Real-world applications

Abel summation methods are used in analytic number theory and in the study of series arising in [signal processing](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3), though the direct applications of this specific theorem are more mathematical than engineering.
