## What it says

Beyond Dedekind cuts and domains, [Dedekind](/mathematicians/dedekind) made several foundational contributions:

- **Definition of an infinite set (1888):** A set is infinite if and only if it can be put into bijection with a proper subset of itself. This is now called "Dedekind-infinite" and is equivalent to Cantor's definition for sets where the axiom of choice holds.
- **Characterisation of $\mathbb{N}$ (1888):** In *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* ("What are numbers and what should they be?"), Dedekind defined the natural numbers axiomatically using what are now called the Peano axioms (Peano published independently one year later).
- **Simply infinite systems:** Dedekind defined what he called "simply infinite systems" — essentially infinite sets with a distinguished element and a successor function satisfying the induction principle.

## History

Dedekind corresponded extensively with Cantor and shared many of the same foundational concerns. His 1872 work on cuts and his 1888 work on the natural numbers together provide a bottom-up construction: from logic to the natural numbers, then to integers, rationals, reals. This programme anticipated the logicist project of Frege and Russell.

## Why it matters

Dedekind showed that the natural numbers can be defined purely in terms of sets and logical operations — numbers need not be primitive objects but can be constructed. This foundational rigour matters because it makes clear exactly what is assumed in arithmetic and analysis, and where the assumptions might fail or need extension.

## Real-world applications

No direct applications — but these foundations underpin all of mathematics.
