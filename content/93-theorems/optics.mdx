## What it says

Newton's *Opticks* (1704) presented two main contributions:

1. **Prism experiments:** White light is not simple but a mixture of all spectral colours. A prism separates them because each colour refracts at a slightly different angle (different wavelengths). Recombining the spectrum with a second prism restores white light.

2. **Corpuscular theory:** Newton proposed that light consists of particles. This was accepted for a century, until Young's double-slit experiment (1801) and Fresnel's wave theory showed light to be a wave. Maxwell's electromagnetic theory (1865) unified optics with electricity, and quantum mechanics (1905, Einstein's photoelectric effect) showed light to be both particle and wave (photons).

**Newton's rings:** interference fringes in thin films of air between a curved glass and a flat surface — a phenomenon that wave optics explains but Newton tried to explain corpuscularly.

## History

[Newton](/mathematicians/newton) conducted his prism experiments in 1666 during the plague years and published them in letters to the Royal Society and eventually in *Opticks*.

## Why it matters

Newton's work established the experimental method in optics and introduced the concept of spectral decomposition — that white light is a sum of components, each with its own properties. This is the physical analogue of Fourier decomposition.

## Real-world applications

Optical physics underlies [MRI imaging](/applications/medical-imaging-mri-ct) (light and magnetic resonance use similar wave physics) and [radar and sonar](/applications/radar-and-sonar) (electromagnetic and acoustic wave propagation).
