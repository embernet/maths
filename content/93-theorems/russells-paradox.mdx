        Let $R = \{ x : x \notin x \}$. Does $R$ contain itself? If $R \in R$, then by the defining condition $R \notin R$. If $R \notin R$, then by the same condition $R \in R$. Either way, contradiction.

## History

Bertrand Russell discovered the paradox in 1901 while reviewing [Frege](/mathematicians/hilbert)'s monumental *Grundgesetze der Arithmetik*, the second volume of which was already in press. Russell wrote to Frege; Frege added a despairing appendix to the proofs. The paradox showed that naive set theory — Cantor's idea that any well-defined property carves out a set — was inconsistent.

## Why it matters

Russell's paradox triggered the foundational crisis of the early twentieth century. The response was a generation of work — type theory, ZFC, Hilbert's programme, Gödel's incompleteness theorems — all aimed at building a foundation for mathematics that could not produce such contradictions.
