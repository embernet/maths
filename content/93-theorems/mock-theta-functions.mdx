## What it says

In his final letter to [Hardy](/mathematicians/hardy) in January 1920, written from his deathbed in India, [Ramanujan](/mathematicians/ramanujan) introduced 17 functions he called **mock theta functions** — $q$-series such as

$$f(q) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{q^{n^2}}{(-q;q)_n^2}$$

where $(a;q)_n = (1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})$ is the $q$-Pochhammer symbol. He observed that they satisfy asymptotic properties "like" theta functions and modular forms near every root of unity, but are not themselves modular forms — he could not explain why.

The mystery persisted for 85 years. In 2002, Sander Zwegers showed that mock theta functions are the "holomorphic parts" of harmonic Maass forms — non-holomorphic modular objects whose non-holomorphic completion restores the modular symmetry that mock theta functions nearly have.

## History

Ramanujan died in April 1920, aged 32. His letter to Hardy contained the mock theta functions with no proofs — only identities and observations. Hardy described them as among the most beautiful and mysterious of Ramanujan's discoveries. The letter was not published until 1988. Zwegers's 2002 Utrecht dissertation finally resolved Ramanujan's question, and the subsequent decade saw an explosion of applications.

## Why it matters

Mock theta functions and harmonic Maass forms have connections to partitions, moonshine (the Monster group), quantum gravity in string theory, and the arithmetic of elliptic curves. Ramanujan had, from intuition alone, identified a new class of automorphic object that mathematicians would not properly understand for nearly a century.

## Real-world applications

Mock modular forms appear in theoretical physics, particularly in the calculation of partition functions in conformal field theory and black hole entropy in string theory.
