        Let $G$ be a group. Then $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of the symmetric group $\operatorname{Sym}(G)$, the group of all permutations of $G$ itself. The isomorphism sends $g \in G$ to the left-multiplication map $L_g : x \mapsto gx$.

## History

Arthur Cayley published the result in 1854. Cayley was a barrister and amateur mathematician who, in his fifties, took the Sadleirian Chair at Cambridge and reshaped group theory and matrix algebra. He had also been the first to write down the abstract definition of a group in a paper of 1854 — the same paper.

## Why it matters

It says that the *concrete* world of permutation groups already contains every possible group. The abstract definition you meet in your first algebra course buys you no extra examples — it buys you a clean language for handling them.

## Where you'll meet it

Early in any second-year group-theory course, often immediately after the definition of a group is given.
