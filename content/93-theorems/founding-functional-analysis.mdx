## What it says

Functional analysis studies vector spaces of functions and the linear maps (operators) between them, treating functions as points in infinite-dimensional spaces. The central objects are normed spaces (especially Banach and Hilbert spaces), bounded linear operators, and the dual space of bounded linear functionals.

The three pillars, all proved by Banach and collaborators:
1. **Hahn–Banach theorem** — bounded functionals extend to the whole space
2. **Open mapping theorem** — surjective bounded linear maps are open
3. **Uniform boundedness principle (Banach–Steinhaus)** — pointwise-bounded operator families are norm-bounded

These three results unlock almost everything in the field.

## History

[Banach](/mathematicians/banach) developed functional analysis in Lwów in the 1920s, working with Steinhaus and others. His 1932 monograph *Théorie des opérations linéaires* unified and systematised the field. The Scottish Café in Lwów, from 1935, became the venue where Banach's circle recorded their open problems in the famous *Scottish Book*. (Note: Banach did not found functional analysis "in a café" — the café was a meeting place, not the birthplace of the field itself.)

## Why it matters

Functional analysis is the mathematics of infinite-dimensional spaces — the natural setting for PDEs, quantum mechanics, signal theory, and optimisation. Before Banach, these areas were studied separately; functional analysis provided a unified language and toolkit. Every PDE course at Year 3 and 4 uses functional analytic methods.
