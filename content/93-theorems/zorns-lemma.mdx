        Formally: let $(P, \le)$ be a non-empty partially ordered set in which every totally-ordered subset (chain) has an upper bound in $P$. Then $P$ contains at least one maximal element.

## History

Max Zorn formulated the lemma in 1935, though Kuratowski had stated an equivalent in 1922. All three of Zorn's lemma, the axiom of choice, and the well-ordering theorem are equivalent. Mathematicians prefer Zorn for arguments that build a maximal something — a maximal ideal, a maximal linearly independent set, a maximal filter — because the chain hypothesis is usually easy to verify.

## Why it matters

It is the workhorse behind every existence proof for "maximal" objects: every vector space has a basis, every non-trivial ring has a maximal ideal, the [Hahn-Banach theorem](/theorems/hahn-banach-theorem) extends bounded linear functionals — all are Zorn's lemma in disguise.

## Where you'll meet it

In abstract algebra and functional analysis whenever someone says "by Zorn's lemma" and waves a hand.
