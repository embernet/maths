## What it says

A group $(G, \cdot)$ is **abelian** if $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ for all $a, b \in G$. Commutativity is an additional structure: not all groups have it ($S_3$, the symmetric group on 3 elements, is the smallest non-abelian group), but abelian groups are far more tractable.

The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups classifies them completely: every finitely generated abelian group is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r$.

Examples: $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$, $(\mathbb{R}, +)$, $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$. The integers under addition are abelian; the rotations of a Rubik's cube under composition are not.

## History

The term "abelian group" was coined to honour [Abel](/mathematicians/abel), whose work on commutativity in the theory of algebraic equations formed the key ingredient in understanding which Galois groups lead to solvable polynomials — precisely, the extensions that can be built by radicals correspond to abelian (or more generally, solvable) Galois groups.

## Why it matters

Abelian groups are the simplest groups, the ones whose structure is completely understood. They are also ubiquitous: $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$ is the group of integers modulo $n$, which underlies modular arithmetic throughout number theory and cryptography. The Pontryagin duality theory of locally compact abelian groups is the abstract foundation of [Fourier analysis](/theorems/fourier-transform).

## Real-world applications

The multiplicative group $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$ for prime $p$ — an abelian group — is the arena for [Fermat's little theorem](/theorems/fermat-s-little-theorem) and hence [RSA cryptography](/applications/rsa-public-key-cryptography). [Error-correcting codes](/applications/error-correcting-codes) and [quantum computing](/applications/quantum-computing-algorithms) algorithms use abelian group structure extensively.

Abelian groups are introduced in core mathematics abstract algebra and are the foundation of specialist areas ring theory, algebraic number theory, and algebraic topology. See the [Structural Turn era](/90-history/07-the-structural-turn).
