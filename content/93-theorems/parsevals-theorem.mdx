        For a square-integrable $2\pi$-periodic function $f$ with Fourier coefficients $\hat f(n)$:

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(x)|^2\,dx = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |\hat f(n)|^2.
$$

## History

Marc-Antoine Parseval published the result in 1799 for trigonometric series; the modern statement on $L^2(\mathbb{T})$ is due to Plancherel (1910). The theorem expresses something physicists already knew empirically: total energy is invariant under the Fourier transform.

## Why it matters

It says the Fourier transform is an *isometry* of $L^2$. The frequency-domain picture and the time-domain picture carry exactly the same total energy. This is the foundation of Hilbert-space methods in signal processing.

## Real-world applications

Bitrate calculations in [MP3 and JPEG compression](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3); SNR estimates in [MRI imaging](/applications/medical-imaging-mri-ct).

In any course that introduces the Fourier transform.
