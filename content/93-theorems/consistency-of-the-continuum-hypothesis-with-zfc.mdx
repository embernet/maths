## What it says

The **Continuum Hypothesis (CH):** There is no cardinality strictly between $|\mathbb{N}|$ and $|\mathbb{R}|$. Equivalently, $|\mathbb{R}| = \aleph_1$ (the first uncountable cardinal).

[Gödel](/mathematicians/godel) proved in 1938 that CH is **consistent with ZFC**: you cannot disprove CH starting from the standard ZFC axioms. He did this by constructing the **constructible universe** $L$ — a minimal model of ZFC in which CH holds.

Paul Cohen proved in 1963 that CH is also consistent with $\neg$CH: you cannot prove CH from ZFC either. Together, these results establish that CH is **independent of ZFC** — it can neither be proved nor disproved from standard axioms.

## History

This was one of the original problems on [Hilbert's 23 problems](/theorems/hilbert-s-23-problems) (1900, Problem 1). Gödel's half was proved using the inner model method; Cohen's half used the technique of **forcing**, invented specifically for this purpose and now a central tool in set theory.

## Why it matters

The independence of CH from ZFC showed that there are natural, meaningful mathematical questions that cannot be resolved by the standard axioms of mathematics. This was philosophically shocking and opened the field of set theory as an area of active research: what should be added to ZFC as new axioms? Different extensions give different answers to CH and to other set-theoretic questions.

## Real-world applications

No direct applications, but the independence results shaped the philosophy of mathematics and the foundations of logic.
