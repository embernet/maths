        Define the action $S = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} L(q, \dot q, t)\,dt$ where $L = T - V$ is the Lagrangian (kinetic minus potential energy). The actual trajectory $q(t)$ between fixed endpoints is the one that makes $S$ stationary — extremal under small variations.

## History

William Rowan Hamilton stated the principle in 1834, building on Maupertuis's earlier "principle of least action" (1744). Hamilton, an Irish prodigy who learned thirteen languages by the age of thirteen, also gave us quaternions and reframed mechanics in the symplectic geometry that bears his name.

## Why it matters

It packages all of classical mechanics into one variational statement. Conservation laws become symmetries of the action via [Noether's theorem](/theorems/noether-s-theorem-symmetry-and-conservation-laws-in-physics). The principle generalises to relativity and quantum field theory (the path integral).

## Where you'll meet it

In any third or fourth-year classical mechanics course.
