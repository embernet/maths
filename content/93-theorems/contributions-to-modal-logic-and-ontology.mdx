## What it says

[Gödel](/mathematicians/godel) made two contributions in this area:

**Modal logic:** Gödel gave an interpretation of intuitionistic propositional logic into modal logic (classical logic with the operators $\Box$ "necessarily" and $\Diamond$ "possibly"), showing that they are related by: intuitionist truth corresponds to classical provability. This is the Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski translation.

**Ontological argument:** Gödel formalised a version of Anselm's ontological argument for the existence of God in modal logic. His formalism proves, within a specific modal system with axioms about positive properties, that a "God-like" being necessarily exists. The argument is logically valid from its premises; the philosophical question is whether the premises are acceptable.

## History

Gödel developed the ontological argument privately in the 1940s but showed it to Dana Scott in 1970 and it was published posthumously. He was reportedly concerned about its reception and did not wish to publish it during his lifetime.

## Why it matters

Gödel's modal work shows that rigorous formal methods can be applied to philosophical arguments. Modal logic — the logic of necessity and possibility — has become important in computer science (temporal logic for program verification), philosophy, and linguistics. The ontological argument formalism, whatever its theological conclusions, is a precise exercise in modal reasoning.

## Real-world applications

Temporal modal logic is used in formal verification of computer systems — checking that a program satisfies properties that hold at all times or eventually.
