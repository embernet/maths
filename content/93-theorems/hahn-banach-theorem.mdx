## What it says

Let $X$ be a normed vector space, $Y \subseteq X$ a subspace, and $f: Y \to \mathbb{R}$ a bounded linear functional with $|f(y)| \leq M \|y\|$. Then there exists a bounded linear functional $F: X \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $F|_Y = f$ and $\|F\| = \|f\|$.

In other words: you can always extend a continuous linear functional from a subspace to the whole space, preserving its norm.

**Consequences:** Every normed space has enough bounded linear functionals to separate points. The dual space $X^*$ is non-trivial. You can find linear functionals that "support" convex sets — the basis of convex optimisation duality.

## History

Hans Hahn proved the theorem for $\ell^\infty$ in 1927; [Banach](/mathematicians/banach) gave the general version in 1929. The theorem requires the axiom of choice (or Zorn's lemma) in full generality — it is not constructive. Banach developed functional analysis systematically in Lwów from the mid-1920s onwards, with his mathematical circle famously working in the Scottish Café from 1935, recording problems in the *Scottish Book*.

## Why it matters

The Hahn–Banach theorem is one of the three pillars of functional analysis (alongside the open mapping theorem and the uniform boundedness principle). It guarantees the existence of dual objects — bounded linear functionals — that are essential for duality arguments in optimisation, control theory, and partial differential equations. Without it, you cannot even prove that continuous linear functionals on infinite-dimensional spaces exist in abundance.

## Real-world applications

[Machine learning](/applications/machine-learning-deep-neural-networks) and [signal processing](/applications/signal-processing-jpeg-mp3) optimisation problems use duality results that trace back to Hahn–Banach. [Option pricing](/applications/option-pricing-finance) risk-neutral measure existence is a consequence of Hahn–Banach-style separation theorems.
