## Definition

- **Definition of an infinite set (1888):** A set is infinite if and only if it can be put into bijection with a proper subset of itself. This is now called "Dedekind-infinite" and is equivalent to Cantor's definition for sets where the axiom of choice holds.
- **Characterisation of $\mathbb{N}$ (1888):** In *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* ("What are numbers and what should they be?"), Dedekind defined the natural numbers axiomatically using what are now called the Peano axioms (Peano published independently one year later).
- **Simply infinite systems:** Dedekind defined what he called "simply infinite systems" — essentially infinite sets with a distinguished element and a successor function satisfying the induction principle.
