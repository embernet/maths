## Definition

The **Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium** in genetics — that allele and genotype frequencies in a population remain constant in the absence of selection, mutation, or drift — was derived by Hardy (and, independently, by Weinberg) in 1908. Hardy reportedly considered it trivial and was somewhat embarrassed to be cited for it. It became a foundational result in population genetics.
