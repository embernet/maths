## Definition

- **$e$** (Euler's number, base of natural logarithm): first used in 1727, published in *Mechanica* (1736). Euler did not name it after himself — that convention followed naturally.
- **$i = \sqrt{-1}$** (imaginary unit): Euler used this notation extensively, though Descartes and others had earlier used $\sqrt{-1}$ without a dedicated symbol.
- **$f(x)$** (function notation): introduced in 1734, codifying the idea of a function as a mapping.
- **$\Sigma$** (summation): introduced in 1755 in *Institutiones calculi differentialis*.
- **$\pi$**: the ratio $\pi = C/d$ had been studied for millennia, but the symbol was popularised by Euler from 1736 (used by William Jones in 1706 — Euler made it universal). Euler did not invent $\pi$ or the concept.
