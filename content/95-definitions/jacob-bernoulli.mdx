## Definition

**Jacob Bernoulli** studied the calculus immediately after Leibniz published it in 1684 and became one of its most skilled early practitioners. He discovered the Bernoulli numbers (appearing in the Taylor series of trigonometric functions and in Euler's work on the zeta function), proved the Law of Large Numbers (the first rigorous result in probability), and formulated the brachistochrone problem: find the curve along which a bead slides from one point to another in minimum time under gravity. He died before seeing the answer.
