## Definition

**Bayesian anomaly detection** models both the normal and anomalous classes explicitly: $p(\text{anomaly} \mid \mathbf{x}) \propto p(\mathbf{x} \mid \text{anomaly})\, p(\text{anomaly})$. The prior $p(\text{anomaly})$ is typically very small (rare events), giving the famous false-positive problem: even a very accurate classifier has low precision when positives are rare. This is exactly the screening paradox of [Medical Diagnosis and Screening](/applications/medical-diagnosis-screening).
