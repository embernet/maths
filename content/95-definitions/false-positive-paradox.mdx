## Definition

A screening test for a rare disease with 99% accuracy sounds impressive. Yet if the disease affects 1 in 1,000 people and you test a random person who tests positive, the probability that they actually have the disease is only about 9%. This counter-intuitive result — the **false-positive paradox** — follows directly from Bayes' theorem and has significant consequences for medical policy on mass screening programmes.
