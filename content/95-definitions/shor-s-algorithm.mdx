## Definition

**Shor's algorithm** reduces integer factorisation to **order-finding**: given $a$ and $n$, find the smallest positive integer $r$ such that $a^r \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$ (the multiplicative order of $a$ mod $n$). If $r$ is even and $a^{r/2} \not\equiv -1 \pmod{n}$, then $\gcd(a^{r/2} \pm 1, n)$ gives a non-trivial factor of $n$ with high probability. This is the classical reduction, requiring only Fermat's little theorem and basic modular arithmetic.
