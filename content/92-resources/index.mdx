A curated list of books, courses, lectures, videos and reference works — useful at any stage, from someone meeting a piece of mathematics for the first time to someone who already does it for a living.

## Books that make mathematics fun

The first few are mind-stretching classics that reward inquisitive readers; the rest are perennial favourites for anyone who enjoys puzzles, paradoxes and the deep patterns connecting mathematics with the rest of thought.

- **Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid** — Douglas Hofstadter. The Pulitzer-winning meditation on self-reference, recursion and consciousness — through the lens of a logician, an engraver and a composer. Slow, playful, indispensable.
- **I Am a Strange Loop** — Douglas Hofstadter. A more direct revisit of the central thesis of *GEB*: that the self is a pattern of self-referential symbols. Read this if *GEB* felt too long; it will pull you back.
- **Metamagical Themas** — Douglas Hofstadter. A collection of his *Scientific American* columns. Every other essay is mathematical wonder — Lisp, the prisoner's dilemma, the lure of recursion, the strange loop in self-reference.
- **The Princeton Companion to Mathematics** — Timothy Gowers (ed.). The single best one-volume tour of mathematics by working mathematicians. Read any chapter; you will know the field better than you did an hour ago.
- **Proofs from THE BOOK** — Aigner & Ziegler. Erdős believed God kept a book of the most beautiful proofs; this is one mathematician's attempt to reconstruct it. Each proof a short joy.
- **The Man Who Loved Only Numbers** — Paul Hoffman. The biography of Paul Erdős, the wandering Hungarian who proved a thousand theorems on other people's sofas. Mathematics as a way of life.
- **Fermat's Last Theorem** — Simon Singh. The 350-year story of the most famous problem in mathematics, ending with Andrew Wiles in his Princeton attic. Readable in a weekend.
- **A Mathematician's Apology** — G. H. Hardy. Hardy's slim, melancholy 1940 reflection on the life and craft of a pure mathematician. The most quoted single book in the canon.
- **Concrete Mathematics** — Graham, Knuth & Patashnik. The book Knuth wrote because he wanted a textbook for the discrete mathematics he needed every day. Sums, generating functions, recurrences — endlessly rewarding.
- **The Art of Mathematics: Coffee Time in Memphis** — Béla Bollobás. Fifty famous problems with surprising solutions. Excellent for train journeys.
- **Letters to a Young Mathematician** — Ian Stewart. Brief, frank advice on a life in mathematics, written as a series of letters. A good companion to Hardy.
- **Chaos: Making a New Science** — James Gleick. The narrative history of dynamical systems, fractals and the rise of chaos theory. Reads like a novel.
- **How to Solve It** — George Pólya. The classic on mathematical thinking and problem-solving heuristics. Short and worth re-reading.

## How to think, study and write mathematically

- **How to Think Like a Mathematician** — Kevin Houston. The transition manual from school-style "solve this" to research-style "prove this".
- **Mathematical Proofs** — Chartrand, Polimeni & Zhang. A gentle but thorough course in proof-writing.
- **A Mind for Numbers** — Barbara Oakley. Cognitive science applied to learning maths and science. The basis of the *Learning How to Learn* course on Coursera.
- **Make It Stick** — Brown, Roediger & McDaniel. General-purpose evidence-based learning techniques; mathematics rewards them especially well.
- **Spivak — Calculus**. A deep, beautiful rigorous calculus text. Demanding.
- **Hardy — A Course of Pure Mathematics**. Over a century old but still readable; the prose is wonderful.

## Free online courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — entire MIT mathematics curriculum, free, with problem sets and exams. The single best free mathematics resource on the internet. [https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Mathematics](https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Mathematics)
- **Khan Academy** — clearest starting point at any level from arithmetic to multivariable calculus. [https://www.khanacademy.org/math](https://www.khanacademy.org/math)
- **Coursera & edX** — well-known university courses on probability, linear algebra, real analysis and statistics. Filter by university.
- **Stanford Engineering Everywhere** — free online versions of Stanford's classic STEM courses.

## Video

- **3Blue1Brown** — Grant Sanderson's animated explainers. The essence of linear algebra, calculus, neural networks, the Riemann hypothesis, and much more. [https://www.3blue1brown.com](https://www.3blue1brown.com)
- **Mathologer** — Burkard Polster's longer-form lectures on beautiful proofs and curiosities. [https://www.youtube.com/@Mathologer](https://www.youtube.com/@Mathologer)
- **Numberphile** — Brady Haran's interviews with mathematicians on a single idea per video. [https://www.numberphile.com](https://www.numberphile.com)
- **Veritasium** — physics-leaning but often pure mathematics; superb on Cantor, Gödel and infinity. [https://www.youtube.com/@veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/@veritasium)

## Reference and history

- **MacTutor History of Mathematics** — the gold-standard biographical archive, maintained at St Andrews. [https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk)
- **Encyclopedia of Mathematics** — the long-running mathematics encyclopaedia. [https://encyclopediaofmath.org](https://encyclopediaofmath.org)
- **Wikipedia mathematics portal** — for quick definitions and good further-reading links. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal:Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal:Mathematics)

## Problem-solving and community

- **Art of Problem Solving** — competition mathematics, Olympiad training, online classes. [https://artofproblemsolving.com](https://artofproblemsolving.com)
- **Mathematics Stack Exchange** — answers from genuine experts to almost any mathematics question. [https://math.stackexchange.com](https://math.stackexchange.com)
- **Project Euler** — a problem set spanning number theory, combinatorics and computation. [https://projecteuler.net](https://projecteuler.net)
- **Brilliant** — interactive courses, free for the curious; paid for the unlimited. [https://brilliant.org](https://brilliant.org)

## Formal proof and verification

Mathematics is increasingly being written so that a computer can check every step. See the [Lean 4 section](/96-lean4) for an introduction to a modern theorem prover, with example proofs you can download and run.

This list is not exhaustive — and it deliberately is not. If you find something better, [add it](https://github.com/embernet/maths/issues).
