Mathematics ran out of Latin letters centuries ago and raided the Greek alphabet — so thoroughly that fluency in mathematics quietly requires knowing a second alphabet. Here are all twenty-four letters, with their usual typecasting. (The casting is convention, not law — but like all typecasting it is remarkably consistent.)

Note that many *capital* Greek letters look identical to Latin ones (capital alpha is just "A") — so mathematics only uses the distinctive capitals: $\Gamma, \Delta, \Theta, \Lambda, \Xi, \Pi, \Sigma, \Phi, \Psi, \Omega$.

| Letter | Name | Say it as | Typical rôles |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | alpha | *AL-fa* | Angles; significance level in statistics; a generic "first" constant. |
| $\beta$ | beta | *BEE-ta* (US *BAY-ta*) | Angles; regression coefficients; the "second" constant to $\alpha$'s first. |
| $\gamma$, $\Gamma$ | gamma | *GAM-ma* | Euler's constant $\gamma \approx 0.577$; paths and curves; $\Gamma$ is the **gamma function**, the factorial's continuous big sibling. |
| $\delta$, $\Delta$ | delta | *DEL-ta* | Small changes: $\delta$ the analyst's tiny wiggle room, $\Delta$ a finite difference ("the change in"). Also the **Kronecker delta** $\delta_{ij}$ (1 if $i=j$, else 0). |
| $\epsilon$ / $\varepsilon$ | epsilon | *EP-si-lon* | *The* small positive quantity of analysis. Two typographic variants of the same letter. Erdős called children "epsilons" — small quantities. |
| $\zeta$ | zeta | *ZEE-ta* (US *ZAY-ta*) | The **Riemann zeta function** $\zeta(s)$ — home of mathematics' most famous unsolved problem. |
| $\eta$ | eta | *EE-ta* (US *AY-ta*) | Efficiency in physics; learning rate in machine learning; a spare small quantity when $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ are taken. |
| $\theta$, $\Theta$ | theta | *THEE-ta* (US *THAY-ta*) | **The** angle, above all others; unknown parameters in statistics ($\hat{\theta}$); $\Theta$ gives exact growth rates in computer science. |
| $\iota$ | iota | *eye-OH-ta* | Rarely used — proverbially small ("not one iota"). Occasionally an inclusion map. |
| $\kappa$ | kappa | *KAP-pa* | Curvature; condition numbers in numerical analysis. |
| $\lambda$, $\Lambda$ | lambda | *LAM-da* | **Eigenvalues**; rate of a Poisson process; wavelength; anonymous functions in programming (the "lambda" of lambda calculus). |
| $\mu$ | mu | *MYOO* | The mean of a distribution; the micro- prefix ($\mu$m); a measure in analysis. |
| $\nu$ | nu | *NYOO* | Frequency in physics; degrees of freedom in statistics. **Lookalike warning:** easily mistaken for an italic $v$. |
| $\xi$, $\Xi$ | xi | *KSY* (or *ZY*) | A spare variable when $x$ and $y$ are busy; random variables in older probability texts. The letter everyone is afraid to pronounce or handwrite. |
| $o$ | omicron | *OH-mi-kron* | Never used — indistinguishable from the Latin o. (Big-O notation is the Latin letter.) |
| $\pi$, $\Pi$ | pi | *PY* | The circle constant $\pi \approx 3.14159$ — the most famous symbol in mathematics; $\Pi$ is the [product sign](/97-symbols/01-arithmetic-algebra-and-comparison). Also, confusingly, projections and permutations. |
| $\rho$ | rho | *ROW* | Density; correlation coefficient; radius in polar-style coordinates. **Lookalike warning:** not a Latin p. |
| $\sigma$, $\Sigma$ | sigma | *SIG-ma* | Standard deviation ($\sigma$) and hence the "six sigma" of industry; permutations; $\Sigma$ is the [summation sign](/97-symbols/01-arithmetic-algebra-and-comparison). |
| $\tau$ | tau | *TOW* (rhymes with "now") | Time constants; torque; proper time in relativity; and the banner of the "$\tau = 2\pi$ would have been the better circle constant" movement. |
| $\upsilon$ | upsilon | *OOP-si-lon* | Almost never used — too easily confused with $u$ and $v$. |
| $\phi$ / $\varphi$, $\Phi$ | phi | *FY* (or *FEE*) | The golden ratio $\varphi \approx 1.618$; angles (the *other* angle, after $\theta$); Euler's totient $\varphi(n)$; $\Phi$ is the normal distribution's cumulative curve. |
| $\chi$ | chi | *KY* | The **chi-squared** ($\chi^2$) test of statistics; the Euler characteristic of topology. |
| $\psi$, $\Psi$ | psi | *PSY* (or *SY*) | The wavefunction of quantum mechanics — physics' most famous letter; a second function when $\phi$ is busy. |
| $\omega$, $\Omega$ | omega | *OH-mig-a* (US *oh-MAY-ga*) | Angular frequency ($\omega$); the last letter, so: sample spaces ($\Omega$) in probability, "the alpha and the omega", ohms in electronics, and lower bounds in computer science. |

## Why Greek?

Partly history — European mathematics grew up on Greek texts, and reverence for [Archimedes](/mathematicians/archimedes) and Euclid came with their alphabet. Partly engineering: a formula needs *visually distinct* symbols for distinct ideas, and a second alphabet doubles the supply. That need has never gone away; when Greek ran out too, mathematicians moved on to Hebrew ($\aleph$), Nordic letters ($\varnothing$), invented squiggles ($\nabla$, $\partial$), and whole new typefaces ($\mathbb{R}$, $\mathcal{N}$, $\mathfrak{g}$). The alphabet-raiding continues — see [where the symbols came from](/97-symbols/06-where-symbols-came-from).

A practical tip for handwriting: the letters that cause the most grief are $\nu$/$v$, $\rho$/$p$, $\omega$/$w$, and $\xi$ (which causes grief all by itself). Exaggerate the differences on paper, and say the names aloud when you read — "nu", not "that v-looking thing".
