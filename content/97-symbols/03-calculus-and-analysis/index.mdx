Calculus notation is a living museum: the symbols in daily use were designed by rival inventors three centuries ago, and the best designs won by making calculation feel inevitable.

## Functions and mappings

| Symbol | Name | Say it as | Meaning & notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $f(x)$ | function value | "f of x" | The output of $f$ at input $x$ — notation by [Euler](/mathematicians/euler) (1734). |
| $f : A \to B$ | function type | "f from A to B" | $f$ takes inputs in $A$ to outputs in $B$. |
| $x \mapsto x^2$ | maps-to | "x maps to x squared" | Defines a function by its rule without naming it (the tail on the arrow is the giveaway). |
| $f \circ g$ | composition | "f after g", "f composed with g" | Do $g$ first, then $f$: $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$. Read right to left. |
| $f^{-1}$ | inverse | "f inverse" | The function that undoes $f$. **Beware:** $f^{-1}(x)$ is *not* $1/f(x)$ — a genuinely unfortunate notation collision everyone just lives with. |
| $\exp$, $\ln$, $\log$ | exponential, logarithm | "exp", "natural log", "log" | $\exp(x) = e^x$; $\ln$ is the logarithm base $e$. Bare $\log$ means base $e$ to mathematicians, base 10 to engineers, base 2 in computer science — always check. |

## Limits and arrows

| Symbol | Name | Say it as | Meaning & notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ | limit | "the limit of f of x as x tends to a" | The value $f$ approaches near $a$. "lim" is due to l'Huilier (1786); the arrow underneath is J. G. Leathem's (1905), spread by G. H. Hardy's 1908 textbook. |
| $x \to a$ | tends to | "x tends to a", "x approaches a" | Inside limits; also "$f(x) \to 3$ as $x \to \infty$". |
| $x \to a^{+}$ | one-sided | "x tends to a from above" | Approach from one side only — from the right ($+$) or left ($-$). |
| $\infty$ | infinity | "infinity" | John Wallis, 1655 — he never explained the shape. Guesses include the Roman thousand (CIƆ) and a closed final omega; nobody knows. In analysis it is a *direction of travel*, not a number. |
| $\varepsilon$, $\delta$ | epsilon, delta | "epsilon", "delta" | The tiny tolerances of rigorous analysis, cast by Cauchy and [Weierstrass](/mathematicians/weierstrass): $\varepsilon$ for the allowed output error, $\delta$ for the input wiggle room. See the worked reading on the [section opener](/97-symbols/00-section-opener). |
| $\sup$, $\inf$ | supremum, infimum | "soop", "inf" | The least upper bound and greatest lower bound — the precise versions of max and min for sets that never quite attain them: $\sup\{x \mid x^2 < 2\} = \sqrt{2}$. |
| $O$, $o$, $\sim$ | big-O, little-o, asymptotic | "big O of", "little o of", "is asymptotic to" | Growth-rate comparisons (big-O: Bachmann 1894; little-o: Landau 1909, who popularised both). $f \sim g$ means $f/g \to 1$; big-O now runs every algorithms course in computer science. |

## Derivatives — four rival notations

Calculus was invented twice, and we inherited everyone's handwriting. All four of these mean *rate of change*:

| Symbol | Whose | Say it as | Where it shines |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ | [Leibniz](/mathematicians/leibniz) | "d y by d x" | Remembers *which* variable; makes the chain rule $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}$ look like cancelling fractions (it isn't quite — but the notation's suggestion is reliably correct, which is its genius). |
| $f'(x)$, $f''(x)$ | Lagrange | "f prime", "f double-prime" | Compact; perfect when the variable is obvious. |
| $\dot{y}$, $\ddot{y}$ | [Newton](/mathematicians/newton) | "y dot", "y double-dot" | Derivatives with respect to *time* — still the house style of physics and engineering. |
| $\partial$ | — | "partial", "curly d" | $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$: the derivative of a many-variable function with the *other* variables held still. Used by Legendre (1786), made standard by Jacobi. |
| $\nabla$ | nabla / del | "grad f", "del" | $\nabla f$ bundles all the partial derivatives into one vector pointing uphill. The symbol is Hamilton's; the name is a Greek harp — [the story is here](/97-symbols/06-where-symbols-came-from). |
| $\Delta$ | capital delta | "delta x", "the change in x" | A finite (not infinitesimal) change: $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$. Calculus is what happens as $\Delta$ dreams of becoming $d$. |

## Integrals

| Symbol | Name | Say it as | Meaning & notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\int f(x)\,dx$ | integral | "the integral of f of x, d x" | Leibniz's elongated **S**, for *summa* — first written in a manuscript dated 29 October 1675. An integral *is* a sum: infinitely many slivers of height $f(x)$ and width $dx$. |
| $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ | definite integral | "the integral from a to b of…" | The signed area under $f$ between $a$ and $b$. |
| $\oint$ | contour integral | "the integral around a closed curve" | An integral around a loop — the workhorse of complex analysis and electromagnetism. |
| $\iint$, $\iiint$ | multiple integrals | "the double/triple integral" | Integration over areas and volumes. |
| $dx$ | differential | "d x" | The ghost of an infinitely small width. Treated informally as a symbol you "integrate against"; made fully rigorous centuries later. |
| $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}$ | infinite series | "the sum from n equals one to infinity" | An infinite sum, defined as the limit of its finite partial sums — the bridge between the [algebra page's](/97-symbols/01-arithmetic-algebra-and-comparison) $\Sigma$ and genuine analysis. |

## Sequences

| Symbol | Say it as | Meaning & notes |
|---|---|---|
| $(a_n)$ or $(a_n)_{n \geq 1}$ | "the sequence a n" | An infinite list $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ — subscripts are positions. |
| $a_n \to L$ | "a n tends to L" | The sequence converges to limit $L$. |
| $\limsup$, $\liminf$ | "lim-soup", "lim-inf" | The largest and smallest values a sequence keeps returning near, even when it refuses to settle. |
