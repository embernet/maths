## The application

Deep neural networks underlie virtually every high-profile capability in modern artificial intelligence: language models that generate coherent text, vision systems that outperform radiologists at detecting tumours, protein structure predictors, and speech recognition. The conceptual core of all these systems is linear algebra and calculus, with the training process being an optimisation problem solved by variants of gradient descent.

## The maths behind it

A **feedforward neural network** with $L$ layers computes a function

$$\mathbf{y} = f_L \circ f_{L-1} \circ \cdots \circ f_1(\mathbf{x}),$$

where each layer applies an **affine transformation** followed by a nonlinearity: $f_l(\mathbf{z}) = \sigma(W_l \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{b}_l)$. Here $W_l$ is a matrix of **weights**, $\mathbf{b}_l$ a vector of **biases**, and $\sigma$ a fixed nonlinear activation function (ReLU: $\sigma(t) = \max(0,t)$ is now standard).

Training minimises a **loss function** $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ — typically mean squared error for regression, cross-entropy for classification — over the parameters $\theta = \{W_l, \mathbf{b}_l\}$. The gradient $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ is computed by **backpropagation**, which is the **chain rule** of calculus applied layer by layer:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{a}_l} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{a}_l}{\partial W_l}.$$

**Stochastic gradient descent** (SGD) iterates: pick a mini-batch of examples, compute the gradient, update $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$. Convergence theory for SGD draws on convex analysis, probability theory, and Lipschitz continuity arguments.

**Singular value decomposition** (SVD) appears in the analysis of transformers: the attention mechanism in large language models computes $\text{softmax}(QK^T / \sqrt{d_k})V$, and understanding its expressiveness requires understanding the geometry of low-rank matrix products.

## A concrete example

GPT-4 has on the order of $10^{12}$ parameters — one trillion weight values. Training required roughly $10^{25}$ floating-point operations over weeks on tens of thousands of GPUs. The core arithmetic is batched matrix multiplication: every forward and backward pass is a sequence of dense matrix products on arrays of size $10^3$–$10^4$.

## History of the application

The mathematical foundations — perceptrons, gradient descent on networks — date to the 1950s–70s, but training deep networks remained impractical until the 2010s when GPU hardware, large datasets, and the ReLU activation combined to make it feasible. AlexNet (2012) triggered the current era; the transformer architecture (2017) enabled the language model revolution.
