## The application

A robot arm must move its tool to a precise location without oscillating or overshooting. A drone must hold position against wind gusts while consuming minimal battery. An autonomous vehicle must follow a lane while avoiding obstacles. All of these are **control problems**: how to choose a sequence of inputs to a dynamical system so that it tracks a desired output. The mathematics is Lyapunov stability analysis, optimal control theory, and the Kalman filter.

## The maths behind it

A dynamical system is governed by ODEs $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})$, where $\mathbf{x}$ is the state (position, velocity, etc.) and $\mathbf{u}$ is the control input. The control law $\mathbf{u} = g(\mathbf{x})$ is chosen to make the desired equilibrium $\mathbf{x}^* = 0$ stable.

**Lyapunov stability**: a function $V(\mathbf{x}) > 0$ with $V(\mathbf{0}) = 0$ is a **Lyapunov function** if $\dot{V} = (\nabla V) \cdot f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) < 0$ along trajectories. If such a $V$ exists, the equilibrium is asymptotically stable — every trajectory starting near $\mathbf{0}$ converges to it. Designing $V$ directly, or using the PID controller (which implicitly builds a Lyapunov function for linear systems), stabilises the system.

**The Kalman filter** solves the state estimation problem: given noisy observations $\mathbf{y}_k = H\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{v}_k$ of a linear system $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = A\mathbf{x}_k + B\mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{w}_k$ (with process noise $\mathbf{w}_k \sim N(0, Q)$ and measurement noise $\mathbf{v}_k \sim N(0, R)$), the optimal linear estimator of $\mathbf{x}_k$ given all observations up to time $k$ is:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + K_k(\mathbf{y}_k - H\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}),$$

where $K_k$ is the **Kalman gain** — the matrix that minimises the posterior estimation variance. The Kalman filter is optimal in the minimum-variance unbiased sense when the noise is Gaussian and the model is linear.

**Linear quadratic regulator (LQR)**: for a linear system, the optimal control law that minimises $\int_0^\infty (\mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{u}^T R \mathbf{u})\, dt$ is the state-feedback law $\mathbf{u} = -K\mathbf{x}$, where $K = R^{-1}B^T P$ and $P$ solves the **algebraic Riccati equation** — a nonlinear matrix equation solvable by numerical methods.

## A concrete example

The attitude control system of the International Space Station uses a combination of gyroscopes (measurement), reaction control thrusters (actuators), and an LQR/Kalman filter control law to maintain orientation to within a fraction of a degree despite disturbance torques from atmospheric drag and gravity gradient. The Kalman filter estimates the full 12-dimensional state vector (orientation, angular velocity, and their biases) at 20 Hz from three gyroscopes and star trackers.

## History of the application

The Laplace transform and frequency-domain control theory were developed by Heaviside (1890s) and formalised by Nyquist, Bode, and others at Bell Labs (1930s–40s). State-space control theory emerged in the 1960s. Rudolf Kalman published the Kalman filter in 1960; it was immediately applied to the Apollo guidance computer and has been central to aerospace navigation ever since.
