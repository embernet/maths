Poincaré is the last mathematician to have worked across essentially all of mathematics. After him, the subject had grown too large for any one person to master; he was the last who could claim to have done so.

His most influential contribution was the creation of **algebraic topology**. Earlier mathematicians had noticed that surfaces and spaces have qualitative properties — how many holes they have, whether they are orientable — that are preserved under continuous deformation. Poincaré put these ideas on a rigorous footing, introducing the **fundamental group** $\pi_1(X)$ of a topological space (which captures loops that cannot be contracted to a point) and developing the theory of **homology** groups.

The **Poincaré conjecture** — that every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere $S^3$ — was posed in 1904. It was the last of [Hilbert](/mathematicians/hilbert)'s problems to be solved, proved by Grigori Perelman in 2003 using Hamilton's Ricci flow programme. It is the only Clay Millennium Prize Problem to have been resolved; Perelman declined both the prize money and the Fields Medal.

In dynamical systems, Poincaré's study of the **three-body problem** (the motion of three gravitating bodies) led to the discovery of **chaos**: that a deterministic system can have solutions that diverge exponentially from nearby initial conditions, making long-term prediction impossible in practice. He introduced the concepts now called the Poincaré map, homoclinic orbits, and sensitive dependence on initial conditions — the essential ingredients of chaos theory, rediscovered in a different form by Lorenz in the 1960s.

His essay "Science and Hypothesis" (1902) and related philosophical writings introduced **conventionalism** — the view that the axioms of geometry and physics are conventions, chosen for convenience rather than reflecting deep truths. The question of whether Euclidean or non-Euclidean geometry describes physical space is, on this view, a question about which framework is most useful, not which is true.

He died in 1912 from complications following surgery, at 58. His collected works fill eleven volumes.
