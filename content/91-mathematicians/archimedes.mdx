Archimedes is perhaps the greatest mathematician and physicist of antiquity. In an era without algebraic notation, without calculus, and without modern numerical methods, he computed areas, volumes, and approximate values of $\pi$ to a precision that would not be matched for centuries.

His calculation of the area of a parabolic segment used a geometric series: the parabola encloses a triangle of area $A$; inside, you can inscribe two more triangles of total area $A/4$; then four more of total area $A/16$; and so on. The total is $A(1 + 1/4 + 1/16 + \cdots) = A \cdot \frac{1}{1 - 1/4} = \frac{4A}{3}$. This is the first convergent infinite series computation in the historical record, accomplished around 250 BCE.

He proved that the area of a sphere is $4\pi r^2$ and its volume is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, and that a sphere inscribed in a cylinder has exactly two-thirds the volume and surface area of the cylinder. He was so pleased with this result that he asked for the figure of a sphere-in-cylinder to be carved on his tombstone.

His approximation $3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}$ (obtained by inscribing and circumscribing regular 96-gons) remained the best known in the Western world for centuries.

The story of his death is told by Plutarch: a Roman soldier commanded him to come, and Archimedes, unwilling to leave his diagrams before finishing, refused. The soldier killed him. Whether true or embellished, it has defined the cultural image of the absorbed, otherworldly mathematician for two millennia.
