Isaac Newton was born prematurely on Christmas Day 1642 (Old Style calendar; 4 January 1643 New Style), a few months after his father died. His mother remarried when he was three and left him with his grandmother; Newton remained emotionally distant from close relationships for the rest of his life.

The plague year of 1665–66, when Cambridge University closed, is the most productive period of isolation in intellectual history. Newton, aged twenty-two to twenty-three, working alone at Woolsthorpe, invented the calculus (the "method of fluxions"), discovered the law of universal gravitation, and developed the theory of optics including the spectrum of white light. He did not publish these discoveries for years.

His *Principia Mathematica* (1687) derived Kepler's three laws of planetary motion from the law of universal gravitation and his three laws of motion. It is one of the most important scientific books ever written — and it was written in the geometric style of Euclid rather than in the new calculus notation, partly because Newton was not convinced that the readers would accept the new methods.

Newton's notation for calculus — $\dot{y}$ for the derivative of $y$ with respect to time — was less flexible than [Leibniz's](/mathematicians/leibniz) $dy/dx$, and the priority dispute between them damaged British mathematics for a century: British mathematicians refused to use Leibniz's superior notation out of nationalistic loyalty to Newton.

His later career was as Master of the Royal Mint: not a sinecure, as it turned out. Newton took the job seriously, oversaw a complete recoinage of the British currency, and personally hunted down coin clippers and counterfeiters, in several cases following the cases all the way to the gallows.

He died in 1727, having never married. He reportedly said near the end: "I seem to have been only like a boy playing on the seashore, and diverting myself in now and then finding a smoother pebble or a prettier shell than ordinary, whilst the great ocean of truth lay all undiscovered before me."
